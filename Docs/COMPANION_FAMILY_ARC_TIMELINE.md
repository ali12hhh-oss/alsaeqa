# Companion Family Arc — Late-Game Timeline

## Core rule
The permanent companion's family rescue is a **late-game arc**. The hero does not begin helping her search for surviving family members in the early stages.

Her family remains an emotional mystery in the background while the early and middle adventure focuses on survival, trust, her gradual ability awakening, the hero's lineage, weapons, and the escalating threat.

## Story pacing

### Early stages — 1–6
- The companion is introduced through the enslavement/rescue arc.
- She focuses on surviving and learning to trust the hero.
- Only very brief fragments of her past may appear.
- No active family-rescue mission.
- No family rescue objective is placed on the map.

### Middle stages — 7–12
- Small clues may appear: a symbol, memory fragment, old marking, or a familiar enemy insignia.
- The companion may react to a clue, but the hero does **not** start a family expedition yet.
- Her abilities continue to awaken through dedicated moments.
- The player can revisit clues later, but they remain optional background mysteries.

### Advanced stages — 13–16
- The family mystery becomes more important.
- The companion begins connecting separate memories and clues.
- A credible indication appears that one or more family members may have survived.
- The companion can independently request that the hero help investigate, but the rescue is still not the immediate main objective.
- This period can reveal why her family was targeted and how their fate relates to the larger enemy network.

### Very advanced stages — 17+
- The hero actively helps the companion search for the remaining members of her family.
- Family locations become full exploration objectives rather than simple clues.
- Rescue missions can involve prisons, convoys, ruined settlements, hidden sanctuaries, or enemy strongholds.
- The companion has meaningful decisions about which clue to pursue and whom to save first.
- Her family arc can intersect with the ancient forces hunting the hero's bloodline.

## Important separation from the hero's quest
The companion's family is **not** simply another chapter of the hero's family search.

- The hero searches for the truth of the Thunder Giants and his own ancestry.
- The companion searches for what remains of her own family.
- Their investigations can eventually intersect because the same enemy network may have hunted both bloodlines.
- The intersection should be revealed late, after both mysteries have developed independently.

## Capture and rescue pacing
The companion may still be captured before the family arc begins. Her capture is about the danger of the world and the enemies' interest in her emerging abilities, not an excuse to start her family quest early.

After a capture/rescue sequence:
- she returns as a permanent companion;
- she can carry new clues or memories;
- the family mystery can deepen;
- the actual family-rescue objective remains locked until the advanced-stage threshold.

## Map and progression rules
Family-related markers should remain hidden or incomplete until the story reaches the appropriate stage.

Early stages:
- no family rescue marker;
- no direct route to a family location;
- only environmental clues or optional memory fragments.

Late stages:
- family clues can combine into a known location;
- the companion can request the investigation;
- new revisitable objectives can appear;
- successful rescues permanently change the world and later dialogue.

## Emotional design
The companion should not repeatedly talk about her family in every stage. Her story should breathe.

Use occasional moments:
- a quiet reaction to a familiar symbol;
- a fragmented childhood memory;
- a discovered object she recognizes;
- a short campfire conversation;
- a decision to keep moving because the time is not right yet.

The player should feel that her family story has been present underneath the adventure for a long time, so that the eventual rescue arc feels earned rather than suddenly introduced.

## Extensibility
The late-game threshold is intentionally not a hard ending. Additional stages can be inserted before, during, or after the family arc without breaking the progression model.

New family members, survivors, enemy facilities, memories, weapons, abilities, and revisitable regions can be added later while preserving this pacing rule.
